How to Take a Screenshot on Any Device

The exact screenshot shortcuts for Windows, Mac, iPhone, Android, and Chromebook — plus where your screenshots go and how to capture a full page.

By Jordan Ellis · Tech How-Tos · 12 min read · Last updated 3 November 2026

Everyone needs a screenshot sooner or later: an error message for tech support, a receipt, a chat you want to keep, a map before you lose signal. The method is different on every device, and the buttons are easy to mix up. Here are the exact shortcuts, device by device, plus where the image ends up afterwards.

The key idea is simple: learn one method well for the device you use most, and one part-screen method for everything else. Whole-screen captures are quick but messy, full of unrelated tabs and notifications. Part-screen captures take two seconds longer and save you cropping, explaining, and embarrassment later. If you remember nothing else from this guide, remember the part-screen shortcut for your main device.

Windows: three ways, pick one

The whole screen, instantly: Press the Print Screen key (often labelled PrtScn or Prt Scr). On most Windows 10 and 11 machines this copies the screen to the clipboard — nothing visibly happens, which confuses people. Open Paint, Word, or an email and press Ctrl + V to paste it. Press Windows + Print Screen instead and Windows saves the image automatically to Pictures → Screenshots.

Just part of the screen: Press Windows + Shift + S. The screen dims and you drag a rectangle around exactly what you want. When you release, the capture goes to the clipboard, and a small notification lets you open it in the Snipping Tool to draw on it, crop it, or save it. This is the method to learn if you learn only one — it avoids screenshots full of unrelated windows and tabs.

The Snipping Tool app: Search for "Snipping Tool" in the Start menu. It offers the same rectangle capture, plus a freehand mode, a window mode (one click captures a single window neatly), and a delayed capture — useful for menus that disappear when you press keys. Set the delay to 3 seconds, open the menu you want to show, and wait.

On some laptops the Print Screen key shares a key with another function, so you may need to hold Fn at the same time. If pressing Print Screen seems to do nothing, try Fn + Print Screen, and check whether a clipboard notification appears. Also note that Windows + Print Screen will not save a file if your Pictures folder has been moved or if OneDrive is set to save screenshots to the cloud instead — in that case, check OneDrive → Pictures → Screenshots.

Once you have a capture in the Snipping Tool, you can annotate it before saving: highlight a button, draw an arrow to an error message, or blur nothing — the tool does not offer blurring, so if a detail must not be shared, crop it out rather than drawing over it. Save as PNG for text and error messages, where sharpness matters, and as JPG only when file size matters more, for example a photo-heavy screen.

Mac: three shortcuts to remember

Mac screenshots are consistent, which makes them easy to learn:

  • Whole screen: Cmd + Shift + 3.
  • Part of the screen: Cmd + Shift + 4, then drag a rectangle. Press the Spacebar after Cmd + Shift + 4 and the cursor becomes a camera — click any window to capture just that window, with a clean shadow around it.
  • The full toolkit: Cmd + Shift + 5 opens a small toolbar with every option, including screen recording and a choice of where files are saved.

By default, Mac screenshots save to the Desktop as PNG files with names like "Screenshot 2026-11-03 at 10.15.00". If your desktop is getting buried, open Cmd + Shift + 5, click Options, and change the save location to a dedicated Screenshots folder.

Two Mac details are worth knowing. First, holding Ctrl while taking a screenshot copies it to the clipboard instead of saving a file — useful when you want to paste straight into a message. Second, Cmd + Shift + 5 also lets you record a selected portion of the screen as video, which is often a better way to show a problem that only appears when you click something. A short screen recording can show support exactly what goes wrong, where a still image only shows the aftermath.

If your Mac screenshots suddenly save somewhere unexpected, it is almost always because the save location was changed in that Options menu, or because a third-party screenshot app has taken over the shortcuts. Check Cmd + Shift + 5 → Options first, then System Settings → Keyboard → Keyboard Shortcuts → Screenshots to see what is actually assigned.

iPhone: two button combinations

Which buttons depend on whether your iPhone has a Home button:

  • Face ID iPhones (no Home button): Press the Side button and the Volume Up button at the same time, then release quickly.
  • Home button iPhones: Press the Home button and the Side (or top) button at the same time, then release quickly.

A small preview appears in the bottom-left corner. Tap it to crop, draw, or add text before saving; ignore it and it saves automatically. Find it later in Photos → Albums → Screenshots.

The timing matters: press both buttons together and let go promptly. Holding them too long can trigger other functions, and pressing one slightly before the other may just change the volume or lock the screen. If you keep getting the lock screen instead, practise the simultaneous press with the phone on a table, so you are not also fumbling your grip.

One iPhone-only trick: on most models you can capture a full web page in Safari. Take a screenshot, tap the preview, choose "Full Page" at the top, and save it as a PDF. It captures the whole article, not just the visible screen — genuinely useful for recipes and directions. Note that the result is a PDF, not a photo, so it will appear in Files rather than in your Photos screenshots album. Share it from there, or print it if you need a paper copy.

For accessibility, iPhone also offers AssistiveTouch and Back Tap (Settings → Accessibility → Touch), which let you take a screenshot with a double- or triple-tap on the back of the phone. That is a genuinely useful option if the button combination is physically awkward for you, and it avoids accidental volume changes.

Android: mostly one combination, with extras

The standard method: Press the Power button and the Volume Down button at the same time, briefly. A preview appears; tap it to edit or share. Screenshots usually land in a Screenshots album in Google Photos or your gallery app.

Samsung extras: Many Samsung phones also let you swipe the edge of your palm across the screen to capture (check Settings → Advanced features → Motions and gestures → Palm swipe to capture). After capturing, Samsung offers "Scroll capture" — tap it and the phone stitches a long page, like a full chat or article, into one tall image.

If the buttons feel awkward: Pull down the notification shade twice and look for a "Screenshot" tile. If it is not there, tap the edit (pencil) icon and add it. One tap beats a two-button squeeze, especially on larger phones.

Android varies more than iPhone because each manufacturer adds its own touches. On Pixel phones, for example, you can also ask the voice assistant to take a screenshot, and recent versions offer a "scroll" option after capturing. On some phones the buttons are Power + Volume Up, or a three-finger swipe down. If the standard combination does nothing, search your phone model plus "screenshot" rather than guessing combinations — one quick check beats ten failed squeezes. Whatever method you use, the result is the same: a PNG in your Screenshots album, ready to crop before sharing.

Chromebook: the key you did not know you had

Chromebooks have a dedicated "Show windows" key in the top row (it looks like a rectangle with two lines beside it, where F5 would be on other keyboards).

  • Whole screen: Ctrl + Show windows.
  • Part of the screen: Ctrl + Shift + Show windows, then drag a rectangle.

Captures save to the Downloads folder and a notification lets you open the image. If your Chromebook has a 360-degree hinge and you are using it as a tablet, press the Power and Volume Down buttons together, like an Android phone.

If you use an external keyboard with your Chromebook, the Show windows key may not exist. In that case, Ctrl + F5 usually acts as the whole-screen shortcut, and Ctrl + Shift + F5 as part-screen. The on-screen keyboard in tablet mode also has a screenshot option. As with other devices, check the Downloads folder first if a capture seems to have vanished — Chromebook screenshots do not go to a Pictures folder by default.

After you capture: three habits that save hassle

Name it or lose it. A folder full of "Screenshot 2026-11-03" files is unsearchable. When a screenshot matters — a receipt, a booking, an error message — rename it immediately: "train-booking-dec-12", "error-code-printer". Ten seconds now saves ten minutes later.

Crop before you share. Screenshots often include more than intended: other tabs, notifications, personal details in the background. Crop to the relevant part before sending, especially for anything going to a group chat or a public forum. On Windows, the Snipping Tool editor does this; on phones, the preview editor does it in one gesture.

Check what is actually in the frame. Before sending a screenshot to support or a colleague, glance at the edges. Status bars, open chats, and browser tabs have all embarrassed people. The part-screen methods above exist precisely so you share only what you mean to share.

Add one more habit: delete screenshots you no longer need. A screenshots folder grows silently, and on a phone it eats storage and clutters photo backups. Once a booking is used or an error is fixed, delete the capture. The ones worth keeping — receipts, confirmations, reference numbers — belong in a named folder, not mixed in with a hundred throwaway captures.

Common mistakes and how to fix them

"Nothing happened when I pressed the keys." On Windows, the capture may have gone to the clipboard with no visible feedback — try pasting into Paint or an email. On phones, the buttons may have been pressed sequentially rather than together. On Mac, check that another app has not taken over the shortcut.

"The screenshot is of the wrong screen." On multi-monitor setups, whole-screen shortcuts may capture all monitors stitched together, or only the main one, depending on the system. Use part-screen or window capture to target exactly one display.

"I cannot capture a menu because it disappears." Use a delayed capture: Snipping Tool delay on Windows, or Cmd + Shift + 5 with the timer on Mac. Set the delay, open the menu, and wait for the capture to fire.

"The image is too blurry to read." Screenshots are only as sharp as the screen. Zoom the page or app to a readable size before capturing, and save as PNG rather than JPG for text. Sending a screenshot through a messaging app that compresses images can also blur it — send it as a file or document if legibility matters.

"My screenshot includes private information." Do not rely on drawing over it with a marker tool that can be removed or that leaves the original underneath in some apps. Crop the private part out entirely, or use an editor that truly covers it, and check the result before sending.

Quick reference

| Device | Whole screen | Part of screen | |--------|--------------|----------------| | Windows | Print Screen (or Win + PrtScn to save) | Win + Shift + S | | Mac | Cmd + Shift + 3 | Cmd + Shift + 4 | | iPhone (Face ID) | Side + Volume Up | Tap preview, then crop | | Android | Power + Volume Down | Varies by brand | | Chromebook | Ctrl + Show windows | Ctrl + Shift + Show windows |

Learn your own device's row and you will never have to photograph your screen with another phone again.

Questions people ask

Where did my screenshot go? Windows clipboard captures go nowhere visible until you paste them; Win + Print Screen saves to Pictures → Screenshots. Mac saves to the Desktop by default. iPhone and Android save to a Screenshots album in Photos or Gallery. Chromebook saves to Downloads. Check those places first, in that order, before retaking it.

Can I capture a whole web page, not just the visible part? Yes, with limits. iPhone Safari offers Full Page as a PDF. Samsung and Pixel phones offer scroll capture for long pages. On a computer, most browsers offer a full-page capture in their developer tools, or through a browser extension — for example, in Chrome, open the developer tools and use the capture command. A PDF or scroll capture is better than stitching several screenshots together by hand.

Why does my screenshot look different from my screen? Colour, brightness, and scaling can differ slightly, and a screenshot taken while a video is playing may show a black rectangle where the video was, if the app blocks capture. Some banking and streaming apps block screenshots entirely for security — that is deliberate, not a fault with your device.

Is it okay to screenshot a private chat and share it? Technically easy, socially risky. Other people did not agree to have their words shared, and a screenshot strips away context. Before forwarding, ask whether you need to share it at all, and if you do, crop out names and details that are not relevant. When in doubt, summarise instead of sharing the image.
